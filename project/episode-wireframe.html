Episode 2: Act and Not Be Acted Upon, Gratitude as the Highest Agency

On a Sabbath morning amid the escalating disruptions of the early pandemic, the mission president teaches the doctrine of agency, contrasting a proactive mindset with a passive, 'cause and effect' mentality. Drawing from 2 Nephi 2 and 11, he illustrates why opposition is structurally essential for spiritual growth. He identifies consistent, pre-emptive gratitude as the most effective vehicle for exercising agency, citing promises from the Doctrine and Covenants and Alma to show that a thankful state naturally invites continuous blessings even during heavy trials.

Transcript

Good morning, elders and sisters. We are living in unprecedented times. As we navigate the complexities of this global situation, I want to talk to you today about something fundamental to our existence: Agency.

Lehi taught that we are free to act for ourselves and not to be acted upon. But what does that mean when the world around us seems to be doing all the acting? When our movements are restricted, when our plans are delayed, how do we remain the 'actors' in our own lives?

"To be grateful is to recognize the hand of the Lord even when the hand of the world seems heavy."

Gratitude is not just a feeling; it is a choice. It is a manifestation of agency. When we choose to be grateful before the blessing arrives, we are exercising the highest form of spiritual agency. We are deciding that our internal state is not dependent on our external circumstances...
